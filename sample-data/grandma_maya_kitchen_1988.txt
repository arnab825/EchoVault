# Oral History: Grandmother Maya's Kitchen Memories
# Recorded: July 20, 1988
# Interviewer: David Rosewood
# Location: Millfield Family Home, Kitchen

[00:01:15] David: "Grandma, what was the first thing you ever learned to bake?"
[00:01:22] Grandmother Maya: "Oh heavens, it was cardamom bread with my mother when I was barely seven years old. We had no electric mixer back then, just a heavy glazed ceramic bowl and a carved birch spoon. You had to knead the dough until your palms were warm and the yeast smelled sweet like morning clover."

[00:02:40] David: "Did you ever burn anything?"
[00:02:45] Grandmother Maya: "(laughs) Everyone burns something! During the winter of 1941, when rations were so tight, I attempted to make barley crumpets over the woodstove. The cast-iron griddle was far too hot, and they turned to solid black charcoal. Your grandfather insisted on scraping the crust off with a pocketknife and eating them with blackberry jam so we wouldn't waste the flour."
